Disclosure wording (question 6)

Adapt the bracketed parts to your practice. Use the same words every time.

In writing, for intake paperwork or your website:

I use AI tools to help with [scheduling, drafting session summaries, preparing handouts]. I review everything before it reaches you. I do not enter [your name together with your health information] into these tools. You can ask me about this at any time.

Spoken, for a first session:

I use a few AI tools to save time on [paperwork and drafts]. Nothing goes to you without my review, and your personal details stay out of them. Ask me anything about it.

Check whether your licensing board or state has its own disclosure requirements. This wording is a starting point, not legal advice.

When an AI tool gets it wrong (question 8)

  1. Stop. Do not use or send that output any further.
  2. Check. Compare it against your own training and a trusted source.
  3. Tell. If it reached a client, correct it with them directly, in plain words.
  4. Record. Note the date, the tool, what went wrong and what you did.
  5. Adjust. Add a review step for that use, or stop using the tool for it.
